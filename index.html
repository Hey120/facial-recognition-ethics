01 · The technology

So what is facial recognition, exactly?

Facial recognition is software that identifies or verifies a person from their face. A camera captures your face, a computer measures it, and those measurements are compared against other faces it already has saved.

Here's the part that surprised us: the system doesn't really "see" you the way a person does. It turns your face into a list of numbers, kind of like a fingerprint but for your face. People call this a faceprint. Two faces "match" when their numbers are close enough to each other.

There are two main ways it gets used, and the difference matters a lot for our project:

1 : 1 · Verification

"Are you who you say you are?"

Your face is compared to one saved face, like unlocking your own phone. You usually chose to set it up.

1 : many · Identification

"Who is this person?"

Your face is compared to a whole database of people, like a store camera checking shoppers against a watchlist. You often don't even know it's happening.

How it works

From your face to a "match" in 4 steps

This all happens in a fraction of a second.

1 📷

Detect

The camera finds a face in the image and separates it from the background.

2 📍

Map

It marks key points like the corners of your eyes, the tip of your nose, and your jawline.

3 🔢

Convert

Those points become a faceprint, a long string of numbers that represents your face.

4 🔍

Compare

The faceprint is checked against saved ones. If they're "close enough," it's called a match.

02 · Why it matters

Why we picked this topic

Honestly, at first we picked facial recognition because all four of us use Face ID every day and never really thought about it. But the more we read, the more it felt like one of the most important tech issues right now.

Most technology collects data you give it. Facial recognition is different because it can collect data just from you existing in public. You can change a leaked password. You can't change your face.

The same technology that unlocks your phone can also get an innocent person kicked out of a store.

And the mistakes don't happen equally. Research has shown that a lot of these systems are less accurate on women and people with darker skin, which means the people who already get treated unfairly are the ones most likely to get misidentified. That's what made us want to look at it through an ethics lens.
